📚 [2026-06-15] Weekly Journal Digest (past 7 days Early Access)

🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

1. ⭐ Spectral and Spatial Harmonization of Landsat ETM+, OLI, and Sentinel-2 MSI Using Time-Series Harmonic Analysis and Blur Kernel Estimation
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment (Vol. 339, Art. 115384, 2026)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115384
   - Develops a unified pipeline for cross-sensor harmonization of Landsat ETM+/OLI and Sentinel-2 MSI via time-series harmonic analysis combined with blur kernel estimation for PSF correction. Addresses both spectral discrepancies and spatial resolution mismatches across the Landsat–Sentinel family.
   - Why it matters: Consistent multi-mission data is the direct prerequisite for pretraining Landsat foundation models on the full HLS archive at scale.

2. ⭐ AgriFM: A Multi-source Temporal Remote Sensing Foundation Model for Agriculture Mapping
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment (Vol. 334, Art. 115234, 2026)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115234
   - Video Swin Transformer FM pretrained on 25M+ global samples from MODIS, Landsat-8/9, and Sentinel-2; synchronized spatiotemporal downsampling preserves phenological signals across sensors. Outperforms general-purpose RSFMs on crop mapping, field delineation, and LULC tasks.
   - Why it matters: First RSE-published evidence that multi-mission Landsat/Sentinel temporal FMs generalize to operational agriculture monitoring.

3. ⭐ Rapid Monitoring of Global Land Change
   - Journal: Nature Communications 16, 8948 (2025)
   - DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-64014-9
   - DIST-ALERT uses HLS (Landsat 8/9 + Sentinel-2A/B/C) at 30 m with 1–4 day tropical revisit to detect near-real-time vegetation loss; high-confidence alerts achieve 94.2% user's accuracy and a mean detection lag of 9.9 days across agriculture, logging, fire, and urbanization events globally.
   - Why it matters: HLS fusion makes near-NRT global vegetation monitoring operational — the most compelling downstream use case for Landsat FM deployment.

4. Deep4Dist: A Deep Learning Dataset for Forest Disturbance Detection
   - Journal: Scientific Data (Nature portfolio, 2026)
   - DOI: 10.1038/s41597-026-07084-8
   - ~17,500 georeferenced patches (500×500 px, 0.2 m resolution) with 20,000+ annotations across four classes: bark beetle (11.2%), clear-cuts (20.4%), windthrow (1.1%), background (67.3%); ResU-Net34 benchmark achieves 88.2% OA, 81.9% F1, 70.3% IoU.
   - Why it matters: First large-scale multi-class annotated dataset for forest disturbance detection, enabling FM fine-tuning and benchmarking for forest monitoring.

5. Forest Carbon Stock and Species Classification from Sentinel-2 Using XGBoost and Conformal Prediction
   - Journal: Scientific Reports (May 2026)
   - DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-50929-w
   - XGBoost outperforms Random Forest and TabNet for carbon stock (MAPE 0.37), species classification (F1 0.83), and age group (F1 0.70) using Sentinel-2; conformal prediction provides calibrated uncertainty at 90% nominal coverage across all tasks.
   - Why it matters: Demonstrates how uncertainty quantification is essential for operational carbon reporting from satellite-derived models.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models

6. ⭐ On the Foundations of Earth Foundation Models
   - Journal: Communications Earth & Environment (2025)
   - DOI: 10.1038/s43247-025-03127-x
   - Proposes 11 essential design features for EO FMs — wavelength embedding, multisensory integration, geolocation encoding, time-variable capability, and physical consistency among them; identifies geographic bias over-representing Europe/NA, and notes that <0.1% of the EO archive is labeled.
   - Why it matters: Canonical design checklist for next-gen EO FMs — directly applicable to any Landsat-based pretraining strategy.

7. Foundation Models in Remote Sensing: From Unimodal to Multimodal — A Survey
   - Journal: IEEE Geoscience and Remote Sensing Magazine (Early Access, 2026)
   - DOI: 10.1109/MGRS.2026.3669086
   - Comprehensive taxonomy covering the field's evolution from single-modal to multimodal architectures as the dominant emerging paradigm; identifies scalability, cross-domain generalization, and physical interpretability as remaining bottlenecks.
   - Why it matters: First IEEE-level peer-reviewed synthesis capturing the full RS FM landscape through mid-2026, marking the field's transition to multimodal pretraining.

8. A Complex-valued SAR Foundation Model Based on Physically Inspired Representation Learning
   - Journal: IEEE Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing (Early Access, 2026)
   - arXiv: 2504.11999
   - Introduces a Scattering Query Decoder that represents SAR pixel scattering as a weighted combination of physically interpretable scattering bases; trained with dual losses aligned to Yamaguchi polarimetric decomposition and power self-supervision. Shows strong few-shot generalization across SAR downstream tasks.
   - Why it matters: Physically grounded pretraining — beyond masking autoencoders — sets a new standard for interpretable, domain-aware SAR FMs and points the way for optical FM design.

📊 This week's journal trend: RSE anchors the week with Landsat–Sentinel harmonization and multi-mission temporal FMs as the operational core of next-gen EO systems; the field is broadly pivoting from unimodal masking-based pretraining toward physically grounded, multimodal architectures.
