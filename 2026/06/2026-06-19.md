🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

1. NAVI-Orbital: First In-Orbit Demonstration of a Zero-Shot VLM for Autonomous Earth Observation `arXiv:2606.18271`
   - Deployed Gemma 3 vision-language model entirely onboard a LEO satellite, achieving 88.16% zero-shot accuracy on the AID benchmark with no fine-tuning; inference runs on satellite-class GPU hardware.
   - A LangGraph-based state machine coordinates detection and dialogue agents, enabling semantic compression instead of raw data downlink.
   - Why it matters: Proves foundation-model-scale intelligence can operate on satellite hardware in orbit, shifting EO from "acquire and downlink" to "process and report."

2. Spaceborne SAR Change Detection and Coherence Analysis for Maritime Port Monitoring `arXiv:2606.18917`
   - Presents a complete SAR processing pipeline (amplitude change detection + interferometric coherence) applied to Tianjin Port, achieving ~0.42m range resolution from spaceborne SAR.
   - All-weather multitemporal analysis identifies vessels, storage structures, and infrastructure changes without optical dependency.
   - Why it matters: Operationalizes SAR-based port monitoring as a fully weather-independent surveillance workflow.

3. A Unified Framework for Efficient Remote Sensing VQA: Adapting Dual, Hybrid, and Encoder-Decoder Architectures `arXiv:2606.19277`
   - Benchmarks PEFT adapter injection across CLIP, BLIP, and FLAVA architectures for RS visual question answering; FLAVA hybrid achieves best multimodal reasoning with <5% trainable parameters.
   - Establishes efficiency baselines for disaster assessment and urban monitoring use cases on high-resolution aerial datasets.
   - Why it matters: Shows lightweight PEFT is sufficient for RS-VQA, lowering the compute barrier for operationalizing VLMs on satellite imagery.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models

4. Externalizing Research Synthesis and Validation in AI Scientists through a Research Harness `arXiv:2606.18874`
   - Introduces Xcientist, a framework that externalizes evidence chains from literature through experiments to final claims, creating auditable records to prevent "claim drift" in automated research.
   - Validated across memory systems, traffic forecasting, and physics-informed neural networks.
   - Why it matters: Directly addresses the accountability gap in AI-driven scientific discovery — a prerequisite for trust in AI scientists.

5. CEO-Bench: Can Agents Play the Long Game? `arXiv:2606.18543`
   - Evaluates LLM agents on 500-day simulated startup operations requiring sustained adaptive decision-making under uncertainty; only Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 maintain profitability above baseline.
   - Exposes that most state-of-the-art models fail at long-horizon coherence despite strong short-task performance.
   - Why it matters: Reveals the fundamental gap between benchmark scores and real-world sustained agency — a critical measure for AI in science workflows.

6. What Must Generalist Agents Remember? `arXiv:2606.18746`
   - Formally proves that near-optimal generalist agents must retain domain-specific memory beyond current observations; links memory capacity to both task disambiguation and transition model reconstruction.
   - Provides a theoretical separation theorem showing memoryless agents cannot achieve sufficiently general performance.
   - Why it matters: Gives rigorous grounding for why memory architectures are non-negotiable for general-purpose AI agents.

💡 One-line comment: Onboard foundation models hit orbit while theory catches up — today's papers bookend the stack from satellite hardware to generalist agent memory.
