🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

1. ⭐ On-board Remote-Sensing Foundation Models for Unsupervised Change Detection of Disaster Events `arXiv:2606.27018`
   - Uses a ResNet-based RSFM paired with an untrained Feature Pyramid Network to detect semantic shifts in latent space between successive orbital passes — no labeled training data required.
   - Achieves training-free disaster change detection on-board satellites, generalizing across terrain types and sensor configurations without task-specific fine-tuning.
   - Why it matters: Demonstrates zero-shot operational change detection via RSFMs — directly previews how Landsat foundation models could be deployed for near-real-time disaster monitoring.

2. EO-WM: A Physically Informed World Model for Probabilistic Earth Observation Forecasting `arXiv:2606.27277`
   - Video diffusion transformer conditioned on meteorological signals (separating baseline climate from weather anomalies) to forecast Earth surface states from satellite time series.
   - Introduces two new benchmarks (Extreme Summer, Seasonal Matched-Pair) and achieves 5.63% relative improvement in NDVI decline prediction accuracy and 7.80% gain in directional fidelity.
   - Why it matters: Bridging weather physics with EO forecasting opens a new paradigm for satellite-based vegetation and carbon cycle monitoring under climate extremes.

3. Topology-Informed Neural Networks for Flood Detection in Optical and Synthetic Aperture Radar Imagery `arXiv:2606.26204`
   - Applies topological data analysis (TDA) descriptors to the SEN12-FLOOD benchmark, showing these features carry independent flood signals and complement ViT-based networks.
   - Provides an interpretable, safety-critical alternative to opaque deep learning for SAR + optical fusion in disaster response.
   - Why it matters: TDA-informed models bring both accuracy gains and transparency to satellite flood mapping — critical for operational use in emergency workflows.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models

4. Socratic Agents for Autonomous Scientific Discovery in High-Dimensional Physical Systems `arXiv:2606.26722`
   - AHOIS embeds Socratic questioning (causal analysis + falsification testing) into a closed-loop multi-agent system to autonomously discover physical hypotheses without pre-existing models.
   - Tested on a multimode-fiber optical platform: independently derives a random-interference encoding hypothesis and achieves ~77% MNIST accuracy with only 16×16 measurements.
   - Why it matters: Critical-interrogation loops now drive genuine experimental discovery — a qualitative advance from agents that merely propose hypotheses.

5. auto-psych: Automating the Science of Mind via Agent-Driven Theory Discovery and Experimentation `arXiv:2606.26460`
   - Nested AI loops — inner loop generates/evaluates probabilistic cognitive models, outer loop designs experiments and collects crowdsourced behavioral data — fully automate theory discovery.
   - Recovers known randomness-perception strategies from synthetic data and produces models that outperform existing psychology literature benchmarks on human subjects.
   - Why it matters: End-to-end closed-loop science from hypothesis to human data to refined model is now demonstrated in a real empirical domain.

6. AI Scientists as Engines of Discovery: A Case for Development within Reformed Institutions `arXiv:2606.22859`
   - Position paper arguing that agentic AI (Denario multi-agent framework) represents a qualitative shift — capable of literature synthesis, hypothesis development, data analysis, and model evaluation.
   - Calls for institutional redesign (verification, authorship standards, interpretability requirements) to govern AI as an epistemic participant, not just a computational instrument.
   - Why it matters: Sets the governance agenda as multi-agent research systems move from demonstrations to operational deployment in scientific institutions.
