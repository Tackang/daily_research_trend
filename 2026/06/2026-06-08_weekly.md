📚 [2026-06-08] Weekly Journal Digest (past 7 days Early Access)

🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

1. ⭐ Derivation and evaluation of Landsat-derived annual aboveground biomass maps for Arctic and Boreal North America, 1984-2022
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment, Vol. 341 (Early Access, Apr 2026)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115446
   - Produces the first continuous 30 m annual Landsat biomass time series from 1984 to 2022 for Arctic and boreal North America, fusing Landsat with airborne LiDAR and U.S./Canadian forest inventory data. Captures wildfire, logging, and permafrost-driven biomass changes at unprecedented temporal and spatial detail.
   - Why it matters: Full-archive Landsat biomass monitoring at landscape scale — the canonical downstream task any Landsat foundation model should replicate operationally.

2. ⭐ Generating an Annual 30 m Rice Cover Product for Monsoon Asia (2018–2023) Using Harmonized Landsat and Sentinel-2 Data and the NASA-IBM Geospatial Foundation Model
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment, Vol. 335 (Early Access, 2026)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115256
   - Fine-tunes NASA-IBM Prithvi on HLS time series to map rice annually across 13 Monsoon Asia countries, outperforming standard deep learning models in cross-region generalization. Automated label generation from existing regional products enables continental-scale mapping without manual annotation.
   - Why it matters: First large-scale proof that HLS-pretrained foundation models surpass conventional approaches for Landsat/Sentinel-2 crop mapping at continental scale.

3. Meta-analysis of North American Arctic and boreal aboveground biomass datasets: assessing accuracy, dynamics, and similarities
   - Journal: Environmental Research Letters, Vol. 21(5) (2026)
   - DOI: 10.1088/1748-9326/ae481a
   - Benchmarks nine satellite-derived biomass datasets across Arctic and boreal regions, revealing systematic biases and divergent temporal dynamics between products. Provides an application-specific decision framework for dataset selection across wildfire tracking, carbon budgeting, and restoration monitoring use cases.
   - Why it matters: Sets the inter-comparison baseline any new Landsat biomass product must beat to claim state-of-the-art in high-latitude carbon monitoring.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models

4. TESSERA: Temporal Embeddings of Surface Spectra for Earth Representation and Analysis
   - Journal: CVPR 2026 (IEEE/CVF, June 3–7, 2026) — ESA endorsed
   - arXiv: 2506.20380
   - Learns per-pixel self-supervised embeddings from Sentinel-1 SAR and Sentinel-2 multispectral time series, handling irregular revisit schedules through novel temporal training. Releases global open-source pixel-wise embeddings for label-efficient land classification, canopy height prediction, and burned area mapping at 10 m resolution.
   - Why it matters: ESA's endorsement and pixel-wise (not patch-wise) design makes it a practical low-compute complement to patch-based HLS foundation models like Prithvi.

5. Foundation Models in Remote Sensing: Evolving from Unimodality to Multimodality
   - Journal: IEEE Geoscience and Remote Sensing Magazine (2026)
   - DOI: 10.1109/MGRS.2026.3669086
   - Surveys the transition from single-sensor unimodal models to cross-modal architectures integrating SAR, optical, hyperspectral, and auxiliary metadata. Includes a practitioner tutorial covering pretraining strategies, fine-tuning paradigms, and benchmark evaluation.
   - Why it matters: The IEEE GRSM reference map for positioning any Landsat or HLS foundation model contribution within the current multimodal foundation model landscape.

📊 This week's journal trend: RSE spotlights full-archive Landsat biomass mapping and Prithvi-powered HLS crop monitoring; CVPR 2026 validates pixel-wise Sentinel foundation models — the field is converging on operational multi-decade EO time series at 30 m, with HLS as the de facto pretraining backbone.
