📚 [2026-06-29] Weekly Journal Digest (past 7 days Early Access)

🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

1. ⭐ Generating an Annual 30 m Rice Cover Product for Monsoon Asia (2018–2023) Using Harmonized Landsat and Sentinel-2 Data and the NASA-IBM Geospatial Foundation Model
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment (Early Access)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115256
   - Leverages the NASA-IBM Prithvi foundation model pre-trained on HLS data to produce annual 30 m rice cover maps across Monsoon Asia for 2018–2023, without per-year retraining. Outperforms traditional classifiers and generalizes across diverse agro-ecological zones.
   - Why it matters: Directly validates HLS-based foundation models for large-scale annual LULC mapping — a template transferable to other Landsat/Sentinel-2 crop types.

2. ⭐ Harmonized Tasseled Cap Transformation Coefficients for Landsat 8 and 9 OLI Sensors Using Surface Reflectance
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment (Early Access)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115384
   - Derives cross-sensor-consistent TCT coefficients (brightness, greenness, wetness) for Landsat 8 and 9 OLI surface reflectance, enabling seamless temporal compositing across the Landsat 8→9 transition. Validated across multiple biomes and land cover types.
   - Why it matters: Removes a key calibration barrier for multi-decade Landsat spectral-change and index-based time-series studies.

3. ⭐ A Framework for Integrating Spatiotemporal Deep Learning Methods with Landsat for Annual Land Cover and Impervious Surface Mapping
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment (Early Access)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115347
   - LCAMS (Land Cover Artificial Mapping System) uses a two-stage architecture: spatial models extract per-year Landsat features, temporal models enforce inter-annual consistency. Piloted as the operational foundation of the next-generation Annual NLCD for CONUS.
   - Why it matters: USGS's adoption of deep learning + Landsat for national-scale operational LULC marks a decisive shift from rule-based to AI-driven land cover products.

4. Mapping Urban Built-up Types from 2000 to 2022 at 10-m Resolution Using Super-Resolution of Landsat Spectral-Temporal Metrics and Center-Patch Classification
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment (Early Access)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115251
   - Super-resolves 30 m Landsat spectral-temporal metrics to 10 m and applies center-patch classification to map fine-grained urban built-up types over a 22-year time series, without requiring Sentinel-2 data.
   - Why it matters: Demonstrates that Landsat's 50-year archive can produce Sentinel-2-equivalent resolution urban mapping, unlocking decadal urban change analysis beyond Sentinel's 2017 availability.

5. Forest Connectivity Boosts Carbon Recovery in Regenerating Atlantic Forests
   - Journal: Communications Earth & Environment (2026)
   - DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-03480-5
   - Using remote sensing and space-for-time substitution across the Brazilian Atlantic Forest, forest connectivity increased aboveground carbon accumulation by 43–69% compared to isolated fragments. Connectivity was a stronger predictor of recovery than forest age or climate variables.
   - Why it matters: Quantifies landscape connectivity as the primary lever for maximizing forest carbon recovery — high relevance for NbS crediting and MRV design.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models

6. ⭐ AgriFM: A Multi-Source Temporal Remote Sensing Foundation Model for Agriculture Mapping
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment (Early Access)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115234
   - Pre-trained on 25+ million globally representative samples from MODIS, Landsat-8/9, and Sentinel-2 using a Video Swin Transformer with synchronized spatiotemporal downsampling. Achieves state-of-the-art on paddy rice, winter wheat, and field boundary delineation across diverse sensors.
   - Why it matters: First multi-source temporal foundation model spanning MODIS+Landsat+Sentinel-2 — architecture and training strategy are directly transferable to a Landsat-centric foundation model.
