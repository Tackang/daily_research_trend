📚 [2026-06-22] Weekly Journal Digest (past 7 days Early Access)

🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

1. ⭐ A Framework for Integrating Spatiotemporal Deep Learning Methods with Landsat for Annual Land Cover and Impervious Surface Mapping
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment (Vol. 338, Art. 115347)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115347
   - Benchmarks spatiotemporal deep learning architectures (ConvLSTM, 3D-CNN, transformer variants) on Landsat Analysis Ready Data for annual 30 m NLCD land cover and impervious surface mapping across CONUS; systematically identifies which temporal-feature approaches best capture multi-year LULC transitions.
   - Why it matters: Operationalizes the full Landsat time-series stack for national annual mapping — directly defines downstream task design for Landsat foundation models.

2. ⭐ Generating an Annual 30 m Rice Cover Product for Monsoon Asia (2018–2023) Using Harmonized Landsat and Sentinel-2 Data and the NASA-IBM Geospatial Foundation Model
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment (Vol. 335, Art. 115256)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115256
   - Fine-tunes NASA-IBM Prithvi on HLS time-series to produce annual 30 m rice maps across all of Monsoon Asia (2018–2023), generalizing across fragmented smallholder paddy landscapes. Outperforms task-specific deep learning across the world's largest rice cultivation area.
   - Why it matters: First large-scale operational validation of a Landsat-based geospatial foundation model for crop mapping in Asia.

3. Global Annual Cropland Dynamics 2015–2024
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment (Vol. 340, Art. 115438)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115438
   - Machine learning on Landsat ARD generates annual 30 m global cropland maps 2015–2024; global extent expanded ~6%, with Africa (+24.5 M ha) and Brazil (+16.5 M ha) leading. Combined with prior work, cumulative expansion since 2003 reaches 14%.
   - Why it matters: First continuous 10-year annual 30 m global cropland record — benchmark dataset for Landsat temporal monitoring at global scale.

4. Small Vegetation Biomass Retention Efficiency Across Global Forests
   - Journal: Communications Earth & Environment (Vol. 7, Art. 116)
   - DOI: 10.1038/s43247-025-03138-8
   - Introduces biomass retention efficiency (BRE) — estimated at 0.21–2.60% globally from satellite observations and ecosystem models — showing only a tiny fraction of photosynthesized carbon accumulates as live structural biomass.
   - Why it matters: BRE provides a satellite-trackable metric to reconcile GPP-based productivity with actual forest carbon stocks in global climate models.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models

5. ⭐ Prithvi-EO-2.0: A Versatile Multi-Temporal Foundation Model for Earth Observation Applications
   - Journal: IEEE Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing (Early Access)
   - DOI: 10.1109/TGRS.2025.3642610
   - Transformer pretrained on 4.2M global HLS samples (30 m, decade-long) with spatial–temporal attention and geolocation/timestamp embeddings; outperforms Prithvi-EO-1.0 by 8% on GEO-Bench and exceeds six competing geospatial foundation models.
   - Why it matters: First peer-reviewed TGRS paper establishing rigorous multi-temporal benchmarks for a Landsat/HLS foundation model.

6. SARCLIP: A Multimodal Foundation Framework for SAR Imagery via Contrastive Language-Image Pre-Training
   - Journal: ISPRS Journal of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing (Vol. 231)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2025.10.017
   - CLIP-style pretraining on 400k SAR image–text pairs with Noise-Robust Encoding and Hierarchical Prompt Learning; achieves 84% recall@1 in cross-modal retrieval and 13% few-shot classification gain over ImageNet baselines.
   - Why it matters: First SAR vision-language foundation model enabling text-guided querying of all-weather SAR archives for land cover and change detection.

7. Adapting Video Foundation Models for Spatiotemporal Wildfire Forecasting via Cross-Modal Progressive Fine-Tuning
   - Journal: IEEE Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing (Early Access)
   - DOI: 10.1109/TGRS.2026.3652453
   - Cross-modal progressive fine-tuning transfers video foundation models to satellite optical time-series for wildfire spread prediction; demonstrates generalization across North American events without domain-specific pretraining.
   - Why it matters: Video foundation models can serve as versatile spatiotemporal backbones for EO prediction tasks, reducing the need for EO-specific pretraining.
