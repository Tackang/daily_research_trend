🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

1. ⭐ GeoRoPE: Ground-Aware Rotary Adaptation for Remote Sensing Foundation Models `arXiv:2606.14760`
   - Lightweight plug-in adapter for multi-sensor RS foundation models that recalibrates token-level positional encodings based on real-world ground sampling distance (GSD). Geo-Coordinate Calibration rescales token-grid offsets; Geo-Frequency Calibration adjusts RoPE frequencies for scene-dependent spatial granularity—both frozen-backbone compatible.
   - Why it matters: A single pretrained RS foundation model can now generalize across Landsat 30 m, Sentinel-2 10 m, and sub-meter sensors without retraining—directly addressing the GSD mismatch bottleneck in multi-sensor pretraining.

2. Machine-Learning Emulation of Satellite Greenhouse Gas Retrievals: Stability over Time `arXiv:2606.09313`
   - ML emulators of GOSAT CO₂/CH₄ satellite retrieval algorithms degrade significantly when applied to out-of-training time periods. Augmenting a Lasso regression with time as an explicit input restores temporal stability, outperforming neural networks validated against TCCON ground truth.
   - Why it matters: Temporal drift in satellite GHG emulators corrupts long-term climate trend analysis—time-aware Lasso is a simple, robust fix for operational monitoring pipelines.

3. CHAM-net: A Contrastive Hierarchical Adaptive Meta-network for Robust Global Methane Flux Prediction `arXiv:2606.00338`
   - Hierarchical encoder–decoder with contrastive meta-learning captures site-specific spatiotemporal heterogeneity in ecosystem methane flux, where the encoder conditions the decoder on historical site characteristics. Achieves R²=0.97 and R²=0.68 on simulated and observational datasets for CH₄ emission and consumption.
   - Why it matters: Accurate global methane flux maps are essential for carbon budget attribution and earth system models—heterogeneity-aware meta-learning closes the site-adaptation gap.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models

4. Toward Generalist Autonomous Research via Hypothesis-Tree Refinement `arXiv:2606.11926`
   - Arbor pairs a long-lived coordinator agent with short-lived executor agents around a persistent Hypothesis Tree that links hypotheses, artifacts, evidence, and distilled insights across iterative research cycles. Achieves 2.5× average gains over baselines and 86.36% on MLE-Bench Lite with GPT-5.5.
   - Why it matters: Persistent structured hypothesis management across agent iterations is the key missing piece for truly generalist autonomous research—Arbor makes it tractable.

5. Deep Research in Physical Sciences: A Multi-Agent Framework and Comprehensive Benchmark `arXiv:2606.18648`
   - PhySciBench introduces 200 physics/chemistry research questions; even Gemini Deep Research achieves only 33.5%. DelveAgent (multi-agent, physics-grounded self-verification) gains 7.5 pp with one-third the compute cost by addressing fragile reasoning chains and poor cross-step knowledge transfer.
   - Why it matters: First rigorous benchmark quantifying how far frontier deep-research agents remain from expert-level physical science—gap is larger than expected, but the architecture fix is tractable.

6. AdsMind: Physics-Grounded Multi-Agent System for Self-Correcting Discovery of Adsorption Configurations `arXiv:2606.19152`
   - Closed-loop framework of LLM agents + machine-learning force field (MLFF) relaxation feedback discovers lowest-energy surface-adsorbate configurations with 100%/98.8% success on AA20/OCD-GMAE62 benchmarks, reducing MLFF relaxation steps by 14× versus baseline. The feedback loop corrects qualitative adsorption-energy sign errors missed by open-loop approaches.
   - Why it matters: Self-correcting agent loops grounded in domain physics dramatically accelerate materials discovery—a concrete blueprint for reliable AI-for-science automation.

💡 One-line comment: GSD-aware adapters unlock true multi-sensor RS foundation models, while physics-grounded multi-agent loops are emerging as the dominant paradigm for trustworthy AI-driven scientific discovery.
