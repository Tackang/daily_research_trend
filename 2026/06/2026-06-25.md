📡 [2026-06-25] Daily Research Briefing

🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

1. ⭐ Changing Modalities: Adapting Remote Sensing Models to New Satellites and Sensors `arXiv:2606.23356`
   - Proposes DeluluNet, which uses "modality hallucination" to predict missing sensor representations, allowing pre-trained RS models to adapt when the satellite sensor configuration changes — substitution, addition, or removal — without full retraining.
   - Trained end-to-end with a single-modality teacher and unlabeled multi-modal data; evaluated across three adaptation scenarios on real satellite datasets.
   - Why it matters: Directly tackles cross-sensor generalization for RS foundation models — critical when Landsat-trained models need to operate on Sentinel or next-generation sensors.

2. Reconstructing GRACE Terrestrial Water Storage with Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Networks `arXiv:2606.23833`
   - Extends GRACE satellite water storage observations back to 1940 for South America by training a GNN on ERA5 meteorological forcing; achieves basin-mean Pearson correlation of 0.94 with fewer input variables than competing methods.
   - Uses a hybrid adjacency matrix combining geographic distance and time-lagged correlations, adapted from a traffic-forecasting GNN architecture.
   - Why it matters: Template for using deep learning to extend satellite-derived geophysical records beyond sensor lifetimes — key for long-term climate trend analysis.

3. High Resolution Sediment-Specific Surface Soil Moisture Retrieval Using Sentinel-1 Time Series and Auxiliary Data `arXiv:2606.24364`
   - Fuses Sentinel-1 SAR backscatter, Sentinel-2 optical, topographic, and temperature predictors for fine-scale soil moisture retrieval at a mining site; best models achieve R²=0.90, RMSE 0.037–0.050 m³/m³.
   - Tree-based ensembles (LightGBM, XGBoost) consistently outperform linear baselines; accuracy varies by sediment type.
   - Why it matters: Advances multi-source Sentinel fusion methodology for operational land surface monitoring at high spatial resolution.

4. RAPID: A Reproducible Multi-Agent Pipeline for Interpretable Disaster Damage Assessment from Satellite and Street-View Imagery `arXiv:2606.21819`
   - Coordinates specialized agents for cross-view damage recognition (satellite + street-level), achieving 0.92 overall accuracy across hurricanes, floods, wildfires, and earthquakes — without task-specific training.
   - Handles domain shift and heterogeneous geospatial data sources through a reproducible agent pipeline with structured damage reasoning.
   - Why it matters: Demonstrates zero-shot multi-agent geospatial intelligence at production accuracy — a new paradigm for autonomous disaster response systems.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models

5. Holistic Data Scheduler for LLM Pre-training via Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning `arXiv:2606.24133`
   - HDS frames training data composition as a Soft Actor-Critic RL problem, jointly optimizing data quality, inter-domain dynamics, and model weight signals to schedule domain mixtures dynamically.
   - Achieves 44% fewer training iterations and 7.2% MMLU 0-shot improvement over baselines; published at KDD 2026.
   - Why it matters: RL-driven data scheduling could directly accelerate geospatial foundation model pretraining where domain heterogeneity is high.

6. SP-Mind: An Autonomous Reasoning Agent for Spatial Proteomics Analysis `arXiv:2606.24235`
   - First autonomous AI agent for end-to-end spatial proteomics — from multiplexed tissue imaging to phenotype discovery via natural language queries — without task-specific fine-tuning.
   - Benchmarked on SP-Bench (102 tasks, 18 categories), outperforming all biomedical agent baselines at state-of-the-art.
   - Why it matters: AI-for-Science agents are maturing from demos to domain-specific benchmarks — a template for future autonomous geoscience analysis pipelines.

7. ReM-MoA: Reasoning Memory Sustains Mixture-of-Agents Scaling `arXiv:2606.24437`
   - Introduces ranked reasoning memory that persistently stores and ranks successful/failed reasoning traces across all MoA layers, routing different agents to distinct trace combinations to preserve exploration diversity.
   - Validated across math, logic, programming, knowledge, and commonsense benchmarks; scaling benefits increase with depth.
   - Why it matters: Solves the depth-saturation problem in multi-agent reasoning — enabling deeper, more reliable pipelines for complex scientific discovery tasks.
