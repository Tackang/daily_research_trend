🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation
1. ⭐ Land cover and flood type govern the detection limits of satellite-based flood mapping `arXiv:2606.07780`
   - Tests Prithvi-EO-2.0 on 19 global flood events (2017–2025); croplands yield best results while tree cover and urban areas fail most — 23 failure modes identified.
   - Engineering improvements outweigh raw model capacity; land cover type is the primary predictor of detection success.
   - Why it matters: Exposes hard failure modes of geospatial foundation models, directly informing robust design for Landsat-scale EO systems.

2. OSMGraphCLIP: Learning Global Location Representations from OpenStreetMap Graphs `arXiv:2606.08046`
   - Encodes geographic areas as heterogeneous graphs (roads, buildings, land-use, POIs) via contrastive learning with spherical-harmonics location encoding.
   - Matches or exceeds satellite-based baselines on land cover classification, biodiversity, climate prediction, and socioeconomic benchmarks.
   - Why it matters: Map-graph embeddings rival pixel imagery, opening a complementary data modality for geospatial representation learning.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models
3. SciTrace: Trajectory-Aware Safety Reasoning for Scientific Discovery Agents `arXiv:2606.08234`
   - Cumulative risk assessment + compositional tool-chain verification across agent pipelines; catches 78.8% of multi-step safety escapes that single-step monitors miss.
   - Evaluated on 240 high-risk research tasks across 6 scientific domains with state-of-the-art safety while preserving output quality.
   - Why it matters: Safety is now a first-class concern as AI agents enter real experimental workflows.

4. ResearchClawBench: A Benchmark for End-to-End Autonomous Scientific Research `arXiv:2606.07591`
   - 40 tasks across 10 domains from real published papers; best autonomous agent scores only 21.5/100, exposing critical gaps in protocol consistency and evidence handling.
   - Why it matters: Sets a rigorous ceiling measurement for how far AI agents are from replacing human researchers end-to-end.

5. Evaluating AI Agents on a Neuroscience Data-to-Discovery Pipeline `arXiv:2606.07718`
   - General-purpose coding agents handle isolated pipeline stages but collapse end-to-end; core bottleneck is inability to evaluate open-ended outputs without predefined criteria.
   - Why it matters: Pinpoints the missing capability — open-ended self-evaluation — blocking autonomous scientific discovery.

💡 One-line comment: Geospatial foundation models and scientific AI agents share the same Achilles' heel today — performance degrades sharply once tasks become open-ended and success criteria blur.
