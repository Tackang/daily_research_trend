📚 [2026-05-25] Weekly Journal Digest (past 7 days Early Access)

🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

1. Photosynthesis, Heat, and Structure: An Evident Hierarchy of Environmental Conditions Driving Wetland Carbon Assimilation
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment, Vol 338 (May 2026, Early Access)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115339
   - Fuses eddy covariance tower data with multi-source remote sensing to build a hierarchical wetland GPP model across diverse wetland types. Reveals photosynthetic activity dominates over thermal and structural signals in driving carbon assimilation variability.
   - Why it matters: Provides a transferable RS-based wetland carbon framework enabling landscape-scale upscaling of GPP from tower networks — directly supports regional carbon MRV.

2. Proximal Sensing of Drought Stress with Sun-Induced Chlorophyll Fluorescence: A Tale of Scaling Up and Down
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment, Vol 338 (May 2026, Early Access)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115370
   - Evaluates SIF across leaf-to-canopy scales to isolate physiological drought stress signals independent of canopy structural changes. SIF responds to drought but NIRv shows comparable sensitivity under certain conditions, setting bounds on SIF's diagnostic power.
   - Why it matters: Defines operational limits of SIF for drought detection, essential for designing satellite SIF retrieval and crop stress monitoring systems.

3. Small Persistent Humid Forest Clearings Drive Tropical Forest Biomass Losses
   - Journal: Nature, Vol 649, 375–380 (2026)
   - DOI: 10.1038/s41586-025-09870-7
   - Analyses 1990–2020 tropical disturbance using ESA CCI + Biomass mission RS products; small (<2 ha) clearings cover only ~5% of disturbed area yet cause ~56% of net aboveground carbon losses in humid forests due to persistent land-use conversion without regrowth.
   - Why it matters: Reframes where RS-based carbon accounting must focus — high-resolution satellite monitoring of small clearings is essential for accurate tropical MRV.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models

4. ⭐ A Framework for Integrating Spatiotemporal Deep Learning with Landsat for Annual Land Cover and Impervious Surface Mapping
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment, Vol 338 (May 2026, Early Access)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115347
   - Introduces LCAMS, a two-stage spatiotemporal DL framework: spatial models extract per-year Landsat features; temporal models enforce inter-annual consistency. Operationalized as the backbone for the new Annual NLCD at 30 m resolution across CONUS, achieving 72.1% overall agreement.
   - Why it matters: The most operational Landsat-based deep learning LULC pipeline published to date — a direct reference architecture for Landsat foundation model fine-tuning and national-scale inference.

5. ⭐ AgriFM: A Multi-Source Temporal Remote Sensing Foundation Model for Agriculture Mapping
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment, Vol 334 (March 2026, Early Access)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115234
   - Pre-trains a Video Swin Transformer on 25M+ global image samples from MODIS, Landsat-8/9, and Sentinel-2 using land cover fraction supervision. Supports crop mapping, field boundary delineation, and LULC tasks across variable temporal lengths and sensors.
   - Why it matters: Shows a unified multi-sensor temporal foundation model (including Landsat) can surpass task-specific models on agricultural RS — a strong blueprint for Landsat foundation model architecture.

6. ⭐ Generating an Annual 30 m Rice Cover Product for Monsoon Asia (2018–2023) Using Harmonized Landsat and Sentinel-2 Data and the NASA-IBM Geospatial Foundation Model
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment, Vol 335 (March 2026, Early Access)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115256
   - First continental-scale application of the NASA–IBM Prithvi model (pre-trained on HLS) fine-tuned for rice mapping across Monsoon Asia. Auto-generated rice training labels from multiple regional products; achieved high accuracy with minimal annotated data.
   - Why it matters: Demonstrates Landsat+Sentinel-2 HLS foundation model fine-tuning at continental scale — the clearest published example of the downstream potential of a Landsat-based FM.

7. Fast Response of Satellite Fluorescence-Derived Plant Physiology to Drought Stress
   - Journal: Nature Communications, Vol 17, Article 2886 (2026)
   - DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-70076-0
   - Integrates satellite SIF, optical vegetation indices, and hydrometeorological data globally to show vegetation physiology responds to drought within ~3 days; structural changes emerge only after ~12 days. Effect is strongest in humid regions.
   - Why it matters: Establishes satellite SIF as a global early-warning indicator for drought impacts on ecosystems, outpacing traditional optical indices by over a week.

📊 This week's journal trend: Landsat-based deep learning pipelines are hitting operational maturity (LCAMS, AgriFM, HLS-FM), while satellite SIF is consolidating as the go-to physiological stress indicator — the convergence of high-cadence multispectral archives and biophysical proxy variables is defining the next generation of RS carbon and vegetation monitoring.
