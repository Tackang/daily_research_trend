🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

1. ⭐ Towards a Foundation Model for the Martian Atmosphere `arXiv:2605.28851`
   - Examines design principles for an ML foundation model of Mars's atmosphere using satellite retrievals, reanalysis data, and physics-based simulations under severe data sparsity.
   - Covers AI-based data assimilation, downstream application design, and strategies for low-label training regimes applicable to Earth EO.
   - Why it matters: Systematic blueprint for domain-specific atmospheric foundation models—directly transferable to Earth observation pretraining with limited labeled data.

2. Sequential Physics-Constrained Neural Operators for Geoscience Simulation `arXiv:2605.28909`
   - Physics-informed Fourier Neural Operators (PINO) achieve R²>0.99 (oil) and R²>0.90 (gas) on the Norne benchmark (113k cells, 30 timesteps), trained in under one hour on B200 GPUs.
   - Ensemble of 1,000 members delivers ~10,000× speedup over conventional finite-volume solvers with covariate-shift correction and spectral stability guarantees.
   - Why it matters: Neural operators as robust real-time surrogates for complex geoscience simulations—enabling scalable Earth subsurface digital twins.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models

3. Frontier LLM Agents Overcome Biological Ontology Curation Bottleneck `arXiv:2605.28965`
   - Claude and GPT agents match human-expert variability annotating phenotypes against standardized ontology terms, substantially outperforming prior NLP methods.
   - Agents operated in structured workspaces with source PDFs, annotation guidelines, and automated validation tools.
   - Why it matters: Agentic AI reaches expert-level scientific curation at scale—unlocks annotation pipelines in ecology and biodiversity data where Landsat-based LULC labeling faces similar bottlenecks.

4. TRACE: Molecular Lead Optimization via Agentic Tool Planning `arXiv:2605.28862`
   - Formulates drug molecular refinement as sequential decision-making over tool trajectories, improving ADMET properties while preserving target binding affinity.
   - Outperforms single-step baselines on success rate, molecular validity, and property improvement across drug discovery benchmarks.
   - Why it matters: Multi-step agentic optimization paradigm generalizes from text tasks into iterative scientific design workflows.

5. BEAMS: Benchmarking and Evaluating AI for Modeling and Simulation `arXiv:2605.28994`
   - Open benchmarks for LLM tools on causal translation, model iteration, and behavior explanation; finds strong qualitative performance but weak quantitative error-fixing.
   - Automated multi-model evaluation via the open-source sd_ai infrastructure reveals no single LLM excels universally across simulation tasks.
   - Why it matters: Identifies concrete capability gaps for AI in scientific M&S workflows—critical before deploying AI in physics-based Earth system models.

6. Multi-regime Failure Modes in Scientific ML `arXiv:2605.29153`
   - Identifies a consistent three-regime training structure across PINNs, neural operators, and neural ODEs; no single optimizer dominates all regimes.
   - Diagnostic framework links failure modes to loss-landscape geometry per regime, validated on multiple PDE benchmarks.
   - Why it matters: SciML training instability is regime-dependent—offers a principled path to optimizer selection for physics-informed geospatial AI models.

7. Beyond Consensus: Trace-Level Synthesis in Mixture of Agents `arXiv:2605.29116`
   - Full reasoning trace aggregation beats majority voting; single-model perturbation-induced variation outperforms heterogeneous multi-model pools on math, science, and coding.
   - Identifies an "aggregation paradox": beneficial corrections exist even under unanimous agent consensus.
   - Why it matters: Consensus voting in multi-agent AI is insufficient—trace-level synthesis is the right unit for building reliable scientific multi-agent systems.
