📡 [2026-05-16] Daily Research Briefing

🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

1. GeoVista: Visually Grounded Active Perception for Ultra-High-Resolution Remote Sensing Understanding `arXiv:2605.14475`
   - Introduces a planning-driven active perception framework that builds a global exploration plan and verifies multiple candidate regions via branch-wise local inspection, aggregating evidence across regions to handle ultra-high-resolution satellite imagery with VLMs.
   - Releases the APEX-GRO dataset reformulating RS tasks as interactive reasoning processes with an Observe-Plan-Track mechanism, showing strong results on RSHR-Bench, XLRS-Bench, and LRS-VQA.
   - Why it matters: Addresses the fundamental bottleneck of VLMs struggling with gigapixel-scale RS imagery — a practical step toward operational RS scene understanding.

2. ⭐ Mini-JEPA Foundation Model Fleet Enables Agentic Hydrologic Intelligence `arXiv:2605.14120`
   - Proposes five 22M-parameter foundation models each trained on distinct satellite modalities (optical, SAR, thermal, phenology, topography-soil) using Sentinel-1/2 and MODIS, rather than a single large planetary-scale model.
   - A routing agent selects the appropriate model per query; cross-validated R² reaches 0.97 for elevation/temperature and 0.81 for precipitation, outperforming monolithic generalist models.
   - Why it matters: Demonstrates that a fleet of compact, modality-specialized foundation models can rival large generalist models — directly relevant to designing efficient Landsat-scale foundation model architectures.

3. AnyBand-Diff: A Unified Remote Sensing Image Generation and Band Repair Framework with Spectral Priors `arXiv:2605.14341`
   - Presents a masked conditional diffusion backbone with a physics-guided sampling mechanism that enforces spectral physical laws (pixel, region, global scales) to generate or repair missing spectral bands in satellite imagery.
   - Overcomes the spectral distortion issue found in standard diffusion models applied to multispectral data, enabling physically accurate Earth observation image synthesis.
   - Why it matters: Physics-aware generative models for satellite data can dramatically augment training sets for downstream tasks like LULC and change detection.

4. Implicit Spatial-Frequency Fusion of Hyperspectral and LiDAR Data via Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks `arXiv:2605.14239`
   - Proposes IFGNet, which uses KAN (learnable spline-based activations) to implicitly fuse HSI and LiDAR in both spatial and frequency domains, capturing nonlinear spectral-geometric relationships.
   - Achieves superior accuracy on Houston 2013 and MUUFL benchmarks while maintaining computational efficiency compared to existing HSI-LiDAR fusion methods.
   - Why it matters: KAN-based implicit fusion offers a new architecture paradigm for multi-modal RS classification, potentially applicable to optical+SAR fusion scenarios.

5. D2-CDIG: Controlled Diffusion Remote Sensing Image Generation with Dual Priors of DEM and Cloud-Fog `arXiv:2605.14326`
   - Uses DEM and atmospheric priors as dual control signals in a diffusion framework with decoupled terrain-atmosphere processing branches and an adjustable cloud-fog parameter for realistic RS image synthesis.
   - Outperforms traditional segmentation/edge-detection conditioned generation in producing naturalistic imagery suitable for training RS AI models.
   - Why it matters: Controllable DEM+atmosphere conditioning enables generation of rare-condition imagery (cloud cover, terrain-specific) to address data scarcity in RS training sets.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models

6. MetaAgent-X: Breaking the Ceiling of Automatic Multi-Agent Systems via End-to-End Reinforcement Learning `arXiv:2605.14212`
   - Addresses the "frozen-executor ceiling" in automatic multi-agent systems by jointly training both designer and executor agents end-to-end via RL with Executor Designer Hierarchical Rollout and Stagewise Co-evolution.
   - Achieves up to 21.7% improvement over baselines, establishing jointly trainable automatic MAS as a new SOTA paradigm for self-designing and self-executing agent systems.
   - Why it matters: End-to-end trainable multi-agent systems could enable fully automated RS analysis pipelines where agent design and execution co-evolve with task complexity.

7. Beyond AI as Assistants: Toward Autonomous Discovery in Cosmology `arXiv:2605.14791`
   - Introduces CMBEvolve (LLM-guided code evolution for quantitative benchmarks) and CosmoEvolve (open-ended virtual lab for data analysis), demonstrating AI agents that autonomously generate and test hypotheses on weak-lensing anomaly detection.
   - Shows that domain-specific agentic systems can produce research-grade diagnostics and improve benchmark scores without human-guided experimental design.
   - Why it matters: Autonomous scientific discovery agents operating on observational data (here, cosmological) offer a direct template for AI-driven Earth observation analysis pipelines.
