🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation
1. Towards Label-Efficient Geospatial Foundation Model Adaptation via Positive-Unlabeled Learning for Building Damage Mapping `arXiv:2611.08561`
   - Parameter-efficient fine-tuning alone doesn't make geospatial foundation models label-efficient. PUMA is a positive-unlabeled framework that needs labels for only a few damaged buildings, and the paper applies it to the 2025 LA wildfires.
   - Why it matters: it shows how to adapt a pretrained EO foundation model when labels are scarce, which is relevant to downstream Landsat FM work.

2. GeoJeV: Unified Spatial Probability for Training-Free Efficient Evidence Acquisition in Ultra-High-Resolution Remote Sensing `arXiv:2611.08879`
   - A frozen model outputs a probability distribution over image regions. That distribution sets search priority, branching width and refinement depth, with no training. It improves Qwen3-VL-8B by 5.32 points on average and runs 7.8× faster than a recent comparable method.
   - Why it matters: it gives a cheap way to run VQA on very large satellite scenes.

3. Arctic-SAR: A Cross-Sensor RADARSAT Constellation Mission and Sentinel-1 Dataset and Fusion Benchmark for Multi-Task Sea Ice Mapping `arXiv:2611.08533`
   - A dataset of 221 co-registered RCM and Sentinel-1 scenes covers ice concentration, stage of development and floe size. Decision-level fusion (77.28%) only matches the Sentinel-1-only baseline (77.26%).
   - Why it matters: it is a caution that adding a second sensor doesn't automatically beat a strong single-sensor baseline.

4. Super-resolved digital terrain models for GEDI geolocation correction `arXiv:2611.09854`
   - A 10 m terrain model is upscaled to 1 m to correct GEDI footprint positions in steep Japanese forests. Track-level shifts match airborne-LiDAR corrections, but per-footprint shifts do not.
   - Why it matters: GEDI footprint errors of about 10 m affect LiDAR-based biomass and canopy-height work, and track-level correction looks usable where airborne data is missing.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models
5. Flex-RSI: Benchmarking Recursive Self-Improvement of Frontier Agents `arXiv:2611.08946`
   - Agents revise their own harness (prompts, tools, memory, control loop) under a fixed compute budget. Gains are largest when the agent builds its own solution, and open-weight models lag on tasks with strict correctness checks.
   - Why it matters: it gives a measurable test of self-improving agents, a building block for automated science.

6. Glyph: An Interpretability Engine for AI-Driven Scientific Discovery in Clinical Data `arXiv:2611.08922`
   - Foundation-model features and sparse autoencoders are turned into coded clinical concepts across 41,628 patients. Of 397 predictive concepts, most associations held in held-out cohorts from other sites.
   - Why it matters: it pairs foundation-model features with interpretability and cross-site checks, a pattern that could carry over to EO.

💡 One-line comment: Today's EO papers focus on adapting foundation models with few labels and cutting inference cost, while agent papers are shifting toward self-improvement benchmarks.
