📡 [2026-07-15] Daily Research Briefing

🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

1. ⭐ TESSERA v2: Scaling Pixel-wise Earth Foundation Models `arXiv:2607.03949`
   - A 395-run scaling study finds that pretraining loss barely predicts downstream performance for Earth observation foundation models, overturning a core assumption. The team releases a distilled 21M-parameter model with Matryoshka representations that outperforms larger proprietary models.
   - Why it matters: Loss-based model selection wastes compute — directly actionable for Landsat/geospatial foundation model development.

2. WeaveEarth: Structured Evidence Construction and Reasoning for Training-Free UHR Remote Sensing Understanding `arXiv:2607.10120`
   - Reformulates ultra-high-resolution remote sensing VLM inference as structured evidence construction with global context constraints, enabling spatial global-local joint reasoning without any model retraining. Achieves consistent gains across multiple RS benchmarks and frozen VLM architectures.
   - Why it matters: Extends VLM capabilities to very-high-resolution satellite imagery at zero training cost.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models

3. IdeaTrail: Full-Process Agent Trajectories for Scientific Ideation `arXiv:2607.10144`
   - Introduces a dataset capturing complete scientific research agent trajectories — from literature search through hypothesis formation and iterative writing — using a two-agent Generator/Advisor pipeline grounded in real published papers. Enables process-supervision training for AI scientific research agents.
   - Why it matters: First end-to-end dataset for training AI agents that can conduct actual scientific research, not just answer questions.

4. Multi-Agent LLMs Fail to Explore Each Other `arXiv:2607.11250`
   - Demonstrates that LLM agents exhibit myopic and polarized interaction patterns in multi-agent tasks, fundamentally failing to probe peer capabilities. Proposes MACE (Multi-Agent Contextual Exploration) with structured peer selection, yielding measurable gains across diverse settings.
   - Why it matters: Identifies a critical bottleneck limiting real-world multi-agent AI performance.

5. Dynamic Agent Skills: A Lifecycle Survey and Taxonomy of Evolving Skill Libraries `arXiv:2607.10113`
   - Surveys 124 papers (2023–2026) on skill libraries for LLM agents, proposing an eight-stage lifecycle from acquisition to governance. Key finding: verifier quality materially affects skill-aware RL; flat retrieval degrades as libraries grow.
   - Why it matters: Canonical reference for building persistent, evolving agent capability systems.

6. AgentAbstain: Do LLM Agents Know When Not to Act? `arXiv:2607.10059`
   - First systematic benchmark (263 tasks, 42 sandbox environments) for agent abstention; even top agents reach only 59.5% accuracy, and abstention ability is independent of task-solving capability.
   - Why it matters: Exposes a fundamental safety gap — agents that can't refuse inappropriate tasks pose real deployment risks.

7. Norm Enforcement for AI Agents: Robustly Shaping Behavior in Multi-Agent Systems `arXiv:2607.09766`
   - Shows basic norm enforcement among competing AI agents is exploitable; proposes reliability tracking and escalating penalties to achieve robust norm compliance across simulated multi-agent environments.
   - Why it matters: Practical foundation for deploying safe multi-agent systems where agent alignment cannot be assumed.

💡 One-line comment: Multi-agent AI is facing its maturity crisis — exploration failures, abstention blindness, and norm violations all surfacing at once.
