📡 [2026-07-29] Daily Research Briefing

🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation
1. ⭐ Fast Fourier Convolutional GAN for 30m Clear-Sky Land Surface Temperature Gap-Free Reconstruction `arXiv:2607.22734`
   - FFC-GAN reconstructs cloud-contaminated 30m Landsat LST using SAR auxiliary data, achieving 0.8-1.8K error even under >70% cloud cover.
   - Why it matters: Directly extends Landsat-scale gap-filling, useful preprocessing for any Landsat foundation model pipeline.

2. Advancing All-Weather Building Damage Mapping to the Instance Level: 2026 Bright Challenge `arXiv:2607.22746`
   - Challenge report on instance-level building damage mapping from pre-event optical + post-event SAR across ~291,000 buildings, 16 disasters.
   - Why it matters: Best solutions still struggle with cross-event generalization, highlighting a key gap for transferable EO models.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models
3. scMIR: A Vision-Language Foundation Model for Single-Cell Light Microscopy `arXiv:2607.22712`
   - Trained on ~208K image-text pairs, scMIR unifies morphology and semantics for cell classification, clustering, and phenotype inference without fine-tuning.
   - Why it matters: Shows vision-language FM recipes generalizing well beyond natural images to scientific microscopy.

4. QFoldAgent: An Autonomous Quantum Optimization Multi-Agent System for Protein Structure Prediction `arXiv:2607.22549`
   - Design, quantum-optimizer, and feedback agents iteratively refine protein folding, lifting structural validity from 87.5% to 98.7%.
   - Why it matters: Concrete example of closed-loop multi-agent systems automating scientific discovery, not just LLM chat.

5. Lexical Discovery in Unknown Environments Orchestrated by Large Language Models `arXiv:2607.22591`
   - LLM-based agent populations play referential games over novel visuals, converging on shared "alien" vocabularies grounded via CLIP embeddings.
   - Why it matters: Demonstrates emergent multi-agent communication protocols, relevant to agent coordination in unfamiliar (e.g. field/sensing) settings.

6. Tokengeist: Multi-Turn Attribution Tracing in Agentic Conversations `arXiv:2607.22610`
   - Recursive DAG-based attribution recovers dependency chains across multi-turn agent dialogues, boosting accuracy from <20% to ~90% over single-pass methods.
   - Why it matters: Addresses "provenance collapse," a core interpretability gap for trusting long-running AI agents.

💡 One-line comment: Today's crop leans hard into multi-agent orchestration (protein folding, lexicon discovery, attribution tracing) as the dominant "AI for Science" pattern, while remote sensing stays focused on gap-filling and disaster-response robustness.
