🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

1. Prior-matched evaluation of operational Earth-observation classifiers: a three-number reporting method demonstrated on Sentinel-1 internal-wave detection `arXiv:2607.07146`
   - Traditional ML classifiers trained on balanced data fail in real-world imbalanced deployment; a three-number reporting framework (balanced-test, operational-prior, post-deployment metrics) exposes this gap with precision dropping from 0.794 to 0.192 operationally. Feature engineering guided by the framework recovered precision to 0.927 while maintaining 0.80 recall.
   - Why it matters: Deployment-aware evaluation is widely neglected in EO research — this three-number protocol should become standard for any operational satellite classifier.

2. Context-Aware Slum Mapping in Sub-Saharan Africa Using Sentinel-1 Texture and Local Climate Zones `arXiv:2607.07532`
   - A combined optical + SAR framework fuses Sentinel-1 texture features with local climate zone (LCZ) information to detect informal settlements in Sub-Saharan African cities. SAR-derived texture features provide seasonal stability that optical imagery lacks, enabling accurate mapping in data-scarce urban environments.
   - Why it matters: Demonstrates SAR's practical advantage for monitoring rapid informal urbanization where optical cloud cover is a persistent challenge.

3. Shifting from Discrete to Continuous Reference Data: QSM-Derived Horizontal Tree Biomass Distribution for Deep Learning Biomass Estimation `arXiv:2607.05260`
   - Replaces traditional discrete plot-level inventory data with spatially-continuous horizontal biomass distribution maps derived from quantitative structure models (QSM) of individual trees. On 100 m² forest plots, estimation error dropped ~17% and R² improved by 0.22 over conventional discrete-reference baselines.
   - Why it matters: Continuous, fine-grained reference data bridges the LiDAR-to-satellite scale gap critical for pixel-level carbon stock monitoring from space.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models

4. Physics-Audited Agentic Discovery in Scientific Machine Learning `arXiv:2607.07379`
   - PA-SciML introduces a verification-first agentic workflow where surrogate models are automatically tested against machine-checkable physics constraints (boundary conditions, causality) before acceptance, catching physically implausible models that pass conventional error metrics. Applied to solid mechanics, it rejects models that low-error baselines incorrectly approve.
   - Why it matters: Agentic scientific ML without physics verification risks confidently wrong predictions — this adds a necessary safety layer for AI-in-science pipelines.

5. From Atomic Actions to Standard Operating Procedures: Iterative Tool Optimization for Self-Evolving LLM Agents `arXiv:2607.07321`
   - EvoSOP enables LLM agents to extract reusable higher-order procedures (SOPs) from their own execution trajectories and iteratively optimize the toolset via construction, merging, evaluation, and pruning cycles. This reduces interaction rounds while improving task success rates over static-toolset baselines.
   - Why it matters: Self-evolving tool libraries are a key step toward scalable autonomous agents that improve with experience rather than requiring manual tool engineering.

6. LLM-Powered Reasoning in Agent-Based Modeling `arXiv:2607.06757`
   - HALE (Hybrid Agent-based and Language-driven Epidemic) replaces static behavioral rules in agent-based simulations with real-time LLM reasoning, enabling adaptive, context-sensitive human behavior modeling. Validated on a COVID-19 simulation in Salt Lake County, showing richer emergent dynamics than rule-based approaches.
   - Why it matters: Marrying scientific simulation with LLM agents opens a path to hybrid models where AI proxies represent heterogeneous human decision-making at scale.

7. Recursive Self-Improvement in AI: From Bounded Self-Refinement to Autonomous Research Loops `arXiv:2607.07663`
   - A survey of 1,250 papers classifies AI self-improvement by what is improved and the degree of human involvement, distinguishing convergent bounded self-refinement (already industrial practice) from open-ended recursive improvement (theoretically constrained). Key finding: self-improvement strength tracks the quality of the evaluator signal, with formal verifiers strongest and self-assessment weakest.
   - Why it matters: Provides a clear taxonomy for where AI self-improvement is reliable vs. where hype outpaces reality — essential grounding for autonomous research agent design.
