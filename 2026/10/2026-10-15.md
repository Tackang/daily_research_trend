🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation
1. Deficit-Driven Stratified Acquisition for Building STRATA-S12: An Open SAR-Optical Corpus for Self-Supervised Learning `arXiv:2610.17680`
   - Builds 740K co-registered Sentinel-1/2 pairs across 192 class-season-region strata. A human-in-the-loop repair step supplied 39.5% of the released pairs.
   - Why it matters: Pretraining-corpus design (balanced strata, strict temporal pairing) carries over directly to a Landsat foundation model.

2. OmniRSI: Taming Latent Diffusion for Unified Multimodal Remote Sensing Image Generation `arXiv:2610.15414`
   - One SD3.5 + ControlNet model generates and translates imagery from incomplete multi-sensor inputs. It ships with RSI-MM, an 11.5M-image text-annotated dataset.
   - Why it matters: It handles missing sensors and modalities, a common problem in multi-sensor time series.

3. RSAJEV: Training-Free Abstaining Prototype Memory for Eliminating Silent Hallucination in JEV-Based Remote Sensing Classification `arXiv:2610.15742`
   - Multimodal LLMs are overconfident on unseen classes (AUROC 0.38). Nearest-neighbor prototype retrieval reaches 89.4% on UCM and 96.9% on AID.
   - Why it matters: A cheap, training-free way to make remote sensing VLMs abstain instead of hallucinating.

4. EarthAlign: Learning Language-Aligned Representations of Earth System Data `arXiv:2610.14624`
   - Pairs 2000–2024 disaster and weather records with captions aligned to 63 ERA5 variables. MeteoLIP, a dual-encoder model, beats CLIP and SigLIP baselines.
   - Why it matters: It brings language alignment to gridded Earth-system data, not only imagery.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models
5. ANASSA: From High-Level Framework to Low-Level Architecture for Spatial Agentic AI `arXiv:2610.14382`
   - Turns a governed geospatial agent framework into an architecture with 47 logical roles, typed contracts, spatial validation, and provenance records.
   - Why it matters: It is a blueprint for reliable, auditable geospatial analysis agents.

6. ACED-Bench: Evaluating How LLM Agents Acquire and Act on Causal Evidence `arXiv:2610.14381`
   - Tests agents on choosing experiments in hidden causal worlds. They often pick the wrong intervention, lose evidence, or fail to stop.
   - Why it matters: It exposes concrete gaps that scientific-discovery agents must close.
