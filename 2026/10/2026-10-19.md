📡 [2026-10-19] Daily Research Briefing

🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation
1. Self-Calibrated Subspace Robust PCA for Parameter-Free Removal of Mixed Noise and Line Stripes in Hyperspectral Images `arXiv:2610.21896`
   - SC-RCCTV estimates noise levels during optimization, so it needs no per-image tuning. It handles Gaussian noise, impulse noise and stripes on real Indian Pines and Urban data.
   - Why it matters: Parameter-free destriping and denoising is a useful preprocessing step for multispectral and hyperspectral EO pipelines, including pretraining data curation.

2. MARS: a foundational map auto-regressor `arXiv:2610.20843`
   - A foundation model that autoregressively generates road networks and building polygons from map data.
   - Why it matters: Vector-map generation is a geospatial foundation-model use case that could pair with raster EO models. It is borderline for this topic, since it works on map data rather than satellite imagery.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models
3. AlphaDiana: A Measurement System for Agents in Verifiable Reasoning Tasks `arXiv:2610.20675`
   - A framework for comparing agent harnesses, tools, skills and memory. It finds that harnesses do not consistently improve accuracy.
   - Why it matters: Agent scaffolding gains may be overstated, so evaluations need controlled measurement.

4. Evolving Together, Failing Apart: The Skill-Policy Co-evolution Trap in Self-Improving Agents `arXiv:2610.20845`
   - Co-evolved agent policies become fragile when their skill banks drift. The paper proposes an audit protocol to catch this.
   - Why it matters: It flags a failure mode for self-improving research agents that accumulate skills over time.

5. SALTO: Solver-Anchored Lifted Transfer Operators for Nonlinear Event Inference `arXiv:2610.21043`
   - Uses Koopman-based operators to infer finite-amplitude sources of nonlinear PDE systems from sensor measurements.
   - Why it matters: Solver-anchored inverse inference is a scientific-modeling approach that could carry over to Earth-system problems.

6. MemComposer: Learning to Assemble Memories for LLM Agents via Reinforcement Learning `arXiv:2610.21091`
   - Learns which retrieved memories to include, how many, and in what order.
   - Why it matters: Better memory assembly is needed for long-horizon autonomous research agents.

💡 One-line comment: Most new EO papers today were already reported, and the new AI-for-science work is mostly about evaluating and hardening agents rather than new models.
