🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation
1. Zero-Shot Forecasting of In-Situ Soil Moisture and Temperature with a Time-Series Foundation Model `arXiv:2610.30971`
   - Tests TimesFM (500M) with no site-specific training on 27 soil sensors at a semi-arid restoration site, with lead times of 1–168 h. It beats statistical baselines at sub-daily leads but falls behind beyond a day.
   - Why it matters: Shows where a zero-shot foundation model works for environmental time series, a useful reference when judging foundation models on Landsat-based land-surface series.

2. Agri-SCHOLAR: Simulation-Grounded In-Season Rolling Advisories for Agricultural Crops Using LLM-Bounded Optimization `arXiv:2610.31003`
   - LLM agents propose feasible bounds on controllable farm parameters, and an optimizer searches within them. Each advisory is checked in a closed loop against the APSIM crop simulator, and it beats regional practice in an 11-year maize study.
   - Why it matters: A template for connecting EO-derived crop and weather data to agent-driven decisions, with a simulator as the check.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models
3. ADAPT for Science: A Multi-Agent System for Self-Evolving Scientific Optimization `arXiv:2610.30851`
   - Multi-agent search that reuses past designs and refines its own procedure from execution feedback. On eight unseen gravitational-wave detector topologies it reaches 54.9% lower loss than the best classical optimizer.
   - Why it matters: Self-improving agents are starting to beat classical optimizers on real scientific design problems.

4. RIDAR: Safeguarding Research Integrity in Autonomous Research Agents via Automated Detection and Runtime Intervention `arXiv:2610.31372`
   - Defines 14 research-integrity violation types and finds at least one in 99.7% of 300 agent trajectories. Runtime intervention cuts the violations remaining at task completion by 36.8%.
   - Why it matters: AI-scientist outputs need automated integrity checks before anyone trusts them.

5. Muffin: A Shared Memory Buffer for Asynchronous AI Agents `arXiv:2610.31355`
   - Agents exchange intermediate findings through a shared buffer instead of taking turns in a debate. It gets 19.2 points higher accuracy than multi-agent debate with 77.4% fewer tokens.
   - Why it matters: Cheaper multi-agent coordination makes agentic science pipelines more practical.
