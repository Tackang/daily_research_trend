🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation
1. RS-OPSD: Reliable Privileged On-Policy-Self-Distillation for Ultra-High-Resolution Remote Sensing VQA `arXiv:2609.38072`
   - Distills the benefit of zoom-in search into the model itself, so ultra-high-resolution satellite VQA needs no runtime tools.
   - The 2B variant beats most 8B models and has the fastest measured inference.
   - Why it matters: Cheaper large-scene reasoning is useful for scaling EO analysis.

2. Hyperspectral Image Models: Technical Report `arXiv:2609.39871`
   - Unifies 55 models across six paradigms in one modular framework with standardized evaluation.
   - Accuracy ranges from 96.4% to 56.7% depending on the dataset, and no paradigm wins everywhere.
   - Why it matters: Dataset difficulty matters more than architecture, so benchmark design deserves more attention.

3. COBICount: Separating Object and Background Responses for Remote Sensing Object Counting `arXiv:2609.39366`
   - Counts objects in aerial images with no target-domain training by isolating object responses from background bias.
   - Trained on buildings, it transfers to vehicles and ships with the lowest MAE of the compared methods.
   - Why it matters: Source-only transfer helps when labels for new targets are scarce.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models
4. Harnessing Domain Specialists in Multimodal Mixture-of-Experts for Efficient Adaptation `arXiv:2610.02123`
   - ExpertLens finds domain-specialized experts directly from pretrained MoE weights.
   - It matches full fine-tuning while updating only 21.7–47.0% of parameters, with 4x faster training, across math, medical, and remote sensing tasks.
   - Why it matters: Cheap adaptation of large models to EO domains.

5. Agent Evaluation Reliability: More Tasks Won't (Always) Fix An Agent Leaderboard `arXiv:2610.00651`
   - Fixed model-scaffold systems rank reliably (0.935–0.994), but ranking the underlying models is much less reliable (0.148–0.841).
   - Scaffold effects limit what extra tasks can fix.
   - Why it matters: Agent leaderboard rankings can mislead model choice.

6. Worse Together: How Performance Breaks Down in Multi-User Multi-Agent Teams `arXiv:2610.00583`
   - Agents serving different users with competing goals do worse than a single coordinating agent in every environment.
   - The coordinator fulfilled roughly twice as many requests in the personal-assistant scenario.
   - Why it matters: Multi-agent designs need centralized coordination to avoid collective failure.
