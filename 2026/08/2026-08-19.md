🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation
1. Task-Driven Three-Layer Distributed Scheduling for Emergency Earth Observation in Large LEO Constellations `arXiv:2608.14789`
   - Proposes a hierarchical scheduling architecture for tasking large low-Earth-orbit satellite constellations under emergency observation demands.
   - Why it matters: better tasking algorithms directly increase revisit frequency and responsiveness for disaster-response imaging.

2. AeroGround: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Aerial-Ground Collaborative Reasoning `arXiv:2608.14722`
   - Introduces a benchmark pairing UAV aerial imagery with ground-view perception for joint reasoning in rescue and infrastructure-inspection scenarios.
   - Why it matters: aerial-ground fusion benchmarks push multimodal geospatial models beyond single-view satellite/aerial analysis.

3. Pushing the Limits of High-Resolution Weather Forecasting through Data Scaling `arXiv:2608.14617`
   - Presents BaguanHR, a super-resolution framework that synthesizes 0.1° global forecasts from 0.25° ERA5 reanalysis, showing power-law scaling gains.
   - Why it matters: data-scaling recipes for Earth-system ML transfer directly to land/carbon monitoring pipelines facing similar resolution bottlenecks.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models
4. Position: AI Agents in Scientific Teams Should Be Studied as Human-Agent Systems `arXiv:2608.14667`
   - Argues AI scientist agents should be evaluated as collaborators embedded in human research teams rather than as standalone autonomous systems.
   - Why it matters: reframes "AI for science" success metrics around team-level discovery outcomes, not solo agent benchmarks.

5. The Hallucination Snowball: Modeling Error Propagation as State Transitions in Multi-Agent LLM Pipelines `arXiv:2608.14588`
   - Models how hallucinations compound across sequential multi-agent pipelines, showing downstream detection rates collapse from 72% to 23.7%.
   - Why it matters: quantifies a concrete failure mode for multi-stage scientific-discovery agent pipelines, motivating stage-wise verification.

6. Do LLM Agents Negotiate Rationally? A Mechanism-Design Framework for Verifiable Multi-Agent Interaction over A2A/MCP `arXiv:2608.14613`
   - Applies mechanism-design theory to test whether LLM agents behave rationally when negotiating over A2A/MCP protocols.
   - Why it matters: verifiable multi-agent coordination is foundational infrastructure for automated research-agent ecosystems.

7. Beyond Correctness: Toward Automated Novelty Verification with Lean 4 `arXiv:2608.14669`
   - Builds a pipeline that formalizes and verifies the novelty (not just correctness) of mathematical theorems using the Lean 4 proof assistant.
   - Why it matters: novelty verification is a missing ingredient for trusting AI-generated scientific discovery claims.
