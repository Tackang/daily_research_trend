📡 [2026-04-28] Daily Research Briefing

🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

1. ChangeQuery: Advancing Remote Sensing Change Analysis for Natural and Human-Induced Disasters `arXiv:2604.22333`
   - Introduces the DICQ dataset pairing pre-event optical with post-event SAR imagery across natural disasters and armed conflicts; ChangeQuery acts as an interactive disaster analyst supporting multi-task reasoning for damage quantification and post-disaster summaries.
   - Why it matters: First framework to unify optical-SAR multimodal change detection with language-driven reasoning for disaster response, pushing the field from visual detection to semantic understanding.

2. Global Remote Sensing Reveals Vegetation Clustering as a Physical Footprint of Shifting Aridity Trends in Drylands `arXiv:2604.22122`
   - Analyzes satellite imagery across eight global dryland ecosystems showing that vegetation spatial morphology (periodic vs. clustered patterns) directly signals whether aridity is worsening or recovering.
   - Why it matters: Provides a non-invasive, satellite-based indicator of dryland ecosystem health trajectories without requiring ground surveys.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models

3. Agentic World Modeling: Foundations, Capabilities, Laws, and Beyond `arXiv:2604.22748`
   - Comprehensive survey of 400+ works unifying model-based RL, video generation, GUI agents, and scientific discovery under a taxonomy spanning physical, digital, social, and scientific law regimes.
   - Why it matters: First principled framework proposing evaluation principles for world models that simulate and reshape environments — a roadmap for AI-driven scientific discovery.

4. Superminds Test: Actively Evaluating Collective Intelligence of Agent Society via Probing Agents `arXiv:2604.22452`
   - Tests collective intelligence of a 2M-agent society (MoltBook) using hierarchical probing agents; society fails to outperform individual frontier models on complex reasoning, with shallow interaction as the core bottleneck.
   - Why it matters: Empirically debunks the assumption that scaling agent count generates emergent collective intelligence.

5. Sound Agentic Science Requires Adversarial Experiments `arXiv:2604.22080`
   - Argues LLM agents optimizing for plausible narratives accelerate invalid scientific claims; proposes a falsification-first standard where agents actively seek to disprove their own findings.
   - Why it matters: A methodological wake-up call for the AI-for-science community — agent-generated results need adversarial stress-testing, not just confirmatory benchmarks.

6. AgentSearchBench: A Benchmark for AI Agent Search in the Wild `arXiv:2604.22436`
   - Constructs a benchmark from 10,000+ real-world agents, revealing that semantic similarity poorly predicts actual execution performance; lightweight behavioral probing substantially improves agent ranking.
   - Why it matters: Addresses the emerging agent discovery bottleneck as ecosystems scale to thousands of specialized agents.

7. Memanto: Typed Semantic Memory with Information-Theoretic Retrieval for Long-Horizon Agents `arXiv:2604.22085`
   - Proposes a memory architecture with 13 typed semantic categories and information-theoretic retrieval achieving ~89.8% accuracy with sub-90ms latency, outperforming graph and vector-based alternatives.
   - Why it matters: Practical memory solution for long-horizon agents without graph overhead — directly applicable to autonomous scientific research agents.

💡 One-line comment: Collective scale alone doesn't create intelligence in agent societies, but multimodal Earth observation is gaining language-driven reasoning for disaster analysis and ecosystem health monitoring.
