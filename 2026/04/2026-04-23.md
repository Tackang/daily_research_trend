🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

1. ⭐ A Proxy Consistency Loss for Grounded Fusion of Earth Observation and Location Encoders `arXiv:2604.18881`
   - Trains a location encoder on abundant geographic proxy variables (e.g., climate, terrain) via a consistency loss, reducing reliance on scarce labeled EO data.
   - Outperforms frozen-embedding and direct-fusion baselines on air quality prediction and poverty mapping, especially out-of-sample.
   - Why it matters: A principled method for geographically generalizing EO models — directly relevant to scaling Landsat foundation models to label-scarce regions.

2. A Controlled Benchmark of Visual State-Space Backbones for Remote-Sensing Segmentation `arXiv:2604.18721`
   - Benchmarks VMamba, MambaVision, and Spatial-Mamba under unified conditions on LoveDA and ISPRS Potsdam datasets, isolating encoder effects.
   - Finds intra-family scaling yields only modest gains; cross-domain generalization is asymmetric; boundary delineation is the dominant failure mode.
   - Why it matters: Encoder scaling alone won't close the RS segmentation gap — boundary-aware decoding is the next architectural priority.

3. ExplainS2A: Explainable Spectral-Spatial Duality Model for Fast Sentinel-2 to AVIRIS-Level Hyperspectral Conversion `arXiv:2604.19007`
   - Reformulates spectral super-resolution as spatial super-resolution via a deep unfolding network + explainable fusion, running at linear-time complexity.
   - Converts million-scale Sentinel-2 images to hyperspectral quality in <1 second, with cross-region and cross-season generalization.
   - Why it matters: Free Sentinel-2 data upgraded to hyperspectral quality at operational scale — broadens downstream material mapping applications.

4. Thermal Anomaly Detection via Physics-Aware Neuromorphic Networks on Sentinel-2 `arXiv:2604.18606`
   - PANN detects bushfire and volcanic thermal events from raw Level-0 or L1C Sentinel-2 data; MCC 0.875 on L1C products.
   - Processes one granule in 2.44 s — faster than Sentinel-2's 3.6 s acquisition interval — with <1 GB memory for onboard deployment.
   - Why it matters: First sub-acquisition-rate real-time disaster detector from an operational optical EO stream.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models

5. AI Scientists Produce Results Without Reasoning Scientifically `arXiv:2604.18805`
   - 25,000+ runs across 8 domains: LLM agents ignore evidence in 68% of cases and revise beliefs in only 26%, despite executing correct-looking workflows.
   - Base model explains 41.4% of performance variance vs. 1.5% for scaffold design; outcome benchmarks cannot detect reasoning failures.
   - Why it matters: Choosing the right base model matters far more than scaffold engineering — a key insight for building reliable science agents.

6. AblateCell: A Reproduce-then-Ablate Agent for Virtual Cell Repositories `arXiv:2604.19606`
   - Two-phase agent: first reproduces published baselines by managing environments and dependencies, then systematically ablates components to find true performance drivers.
   - Achieves 88.9% workflow completion and 93.3% component identification accuracy across three biology repositories, beating human experts.
   - Why it matters: Automated scientific verification at scale — a template for AI-driven reproducibility in complex computational science.

7. SHARP: A Scientific Human-Agent Reproduction Pipeline `arXiv:2604.18752`
   - Decomposes scientific reproduction into agent-executed steps (code gen, testing, QA subagents) with human checkpoints at key decision points.
   - Demonstrated on a particle physics jet classification analysis, evaluated on performance, code quality, and human-agent interaction patterns.
   - Why it matters: Human-in-the-loop agentic reproduction preserves scientific judgment while offloading implementation work to AI.

💡 One-line comment: Agents are fast at executing science workflows but still don't reason like scientists — today's papers confirm that base model quality and human oversight are the two non-negotiable levers.
