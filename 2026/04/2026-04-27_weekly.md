📚 [2026-04-27] Weekly Journal Digest (past 7 days Early Access)

🔬 Remote Sensing & Earth Observation

1. ⭐ Mapping Three Decades of Forest Structural Changes in Japan Using Landsat Time Series and Airborne LiDAR Data
   - Journal: ISPRS J. Photogrammetry & Remote Sensing (Vol. 234, Apr 2026)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2026.02.022
   - Shimizu et al. use 30 years of Landsat imagery (1990–2020) fused with airborne LiDAR data to track national-scale forest structural change across major forest types in Japan. Disturbance and recovery trajectories are separated at 30 m resolution over the full archive period.
   - Why it matters: Demonstrates Landsat's multi-decade archive as the irreplaceable backbone for long-term forest structure monitoring — directly informs temporal pretraining strategies for a Landsat foundation model.

2. Leveraging sUAS-Sentinel-2 Synergy for Cross-Scale Mapping of Canopy Cover and Aboveground Biomass Across Mongolia and Kazakhstan
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment (Vol. 336, Apr 2026)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115302
   - Kolluru et al. integrate drone-derived structural metrics with Sentinel-2 spectral features to map canopy cover and aboveground biomass across Central Asian drylands. Cross-scale transfer extends sub-meter drone training data to 10 m satellite predictions with minimal ground truth.
   - Why it matters: Scalable biomass pipeline generalizable to data-sparse dryland regions — a template for label-efficient satellite-scale inference.

3. Multi-Satellite Data Fusion for Improved Field-Scale Evapotranspiration Mapping on Google Earth Engine
   - Journal: Remote Sensing of Environment (Vol. 336, Apr 2026)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2026.115299
   - Liu et al. blend Landsat, MODIS, and Sentinel-2 time series on Google Earth Engine to produce continuous 30 m evapotranspiration estimates, filling Landsat's temporal gaps with MODIS-derived phenology while retaining Landsat spatial detail.
   - Why it matters: Operational multi-sensor fusion framework validating complementary roles of Landsat and Sentinel for high-resolution agricultural water-use monitoring.

4. Global Mapping of Tidal Wetlands and Adjacent Environments Using Tidal Analysis and Multi-Source Earth Observations
   - Journal: ISPRS J. Photogrammetry & Remote Sensing (Vol. 234, Apr 2026)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2026.02.014
   - Li et al. fuse Landsat, Sentinel-2, and SAR time series with tidal height modeling to produce the first global 30 m tidal wetland inventory, disentangling intertidal, supratidal, and adjacent upland land-cover classes.
   - Why it matters: Foundational global dataset enabling blue carbon stock estimation and coastal LULC change monitoring at scale.

5. STNet: A Spatio-Temporal Network for Binary Change Detection in High-Resolution Remote Sensing Images
   - Journal: ISPRS J. Photogrammetry & Remote Sensing (Vol. 234, Apr 2026)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2026.02.003
   - Lu et al. propose STNet, a dual-stream encoder that jointly captures spatial context and temporal difference signals from bitemporal RS image pairs. It achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on multiple public change detection benchmarks.
   - Why it matters: Lightweight change detection backbone readily paired with time-series foundation models for large-scale LULC change monitoring.

🤖 AI & Foundation Models

6. ⭐ SpecAware: A Spectral-Content Aware Foundation Model for Unifying Multi-Sensor Learning in Hyperspectral Remote Sensing Mapping
   - Journal: ISPRS J. Photogrammetry & Remote Sensing (Vol. 234, Apr 2026)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2026.02.024
   - Ji et al. pre-train SpecAware on mixed hyperspectral and multispectral imagery with spectral-adaptive modules that dynamically re-weight band contributions, enabling zero-shot and few-shot transfer to new sensor configurations without full retraining.
   - Why it matters: Directly addresses the multi-sensor unification challenge at the core of a Landsat+Sentinel foundation model — spectral-content awareness is a key architectural ingredient.

7. Quantitative Comparison of Fine-Tuning Techniques for Pretrained Latent Diffusion Models in the Generation of Unseen SAR Images
   - Journal: ISPRS J. Photogrammetry & Remote Sensing (Vol. 234, Apr 2026)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2026.02.018
   - Debuysère et al. benchmark LoRA, DreamBooth, and textual inversion for adapting pretrained latent diffusion models to synthesize SAR imagery from optical inputs, systematically evaluating parameter efficiency, adaptation speed, and image fidelity.
   - Why it matters: Establishes fine-tuning best practices for geospatial generative models — critical reference for efficient domain adaptation of RS foundation models.

8. Epistemic and Aleatoric Uncertainty in Optical Vegetation Trait Retrieval: Concepts, Methods, and Outlook
   - Journal: ISPRS J. Photogrammetry & Remote Sensing (Vol. 234, Apr 2026)
   - DOI: 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2026.02.020
   - Verrelst et al. review Bayesian deep learning, MC dropout, and ensemble methods for uncertainty quantification in ML-based vegetation trait retrieval from optical imagery, proposing a pipeline for propagating uncertainty through to carbon and biomass products.
   - Why it matters: Uncertainty-awareness is a prerequisite for operationally trustworthy AI in policy-grade carbon monitoring workflows.

📊 This week's journal trend: ISPRS Vol. 234 and RSE Vol. 336 converge on two themes — multi-sensor fusion at 30 m scale for carbon/biomass applications, and spectral-adaptive foundation models that generalize across sensor types without full retraining.
